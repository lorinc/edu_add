## How to do every block

**Reading (English, science, history):**

1. Read the part once, all the way through, without notes.
2. Read it again with your doc open. Write the 3-5 most important ideas as bullet points, **in your own words**.
3. Answer the questions the day plan lists.

**Answering a question:**

1. Look at the reading for the part you need.
2. Close it.
3. Write the answer from memory, in full sentences, in your own words.
4. Can't do it without looking? Then you don't know it yet. Read again, close, write. That's not wasted time: that's the learning.

A good answer says **why**, not just **what**, and uses a real example from the reading when the question asks for one.

**Maths:** follow your [plan](#maths). Every problem gets written working in your maths notebook, one step per line, before you type the answer. Photo of the notebook pages into your doc at the end of the block.

**Type it yourself.** No pasting, no AI, no translator. Quotes are fine, in quotation marks. *Why: the learning happens in the effort of putting it into your own words. A pasted answer is an hour of work with nothing left in your head.*

**Done means everything in the "Done when" column is in your doc by the checkpoint.**

## What a self-run day looks like

A self-run day is a day you ran yourself. All six:

- ☐ Day sheet filled in by 10:00.
- ☐ Everything due by the checkpoint is done (maths and English or science; on full support that's 13:00).
- ☐ At the teach-back, you can explain your work in your own words. One "I'm not sure, but I think..." is fine.
- ☐ Self-marking done.
- ☐ PE done (family PE at 17:15; if it doesn't happen, it doesn't count against you).
- ☐ Any shortcut, you flagged yourself and fixed.

Every self-run day is a dark green square on the [progress board](index.html). **Rank 1** comes with 5 self-run days out of your last 6. **Rank 2** with 10 out of 12. A rank, once earned, is yours: it never goes down. What each rank gives you is in [the routine](../../curriculum/routine.md#ranks).

A day that isn't self-run is just a day. It doesn't erase anything. The squares and totals only grow.

## The checkpoint

At 13:00 (full support) your parent will:

1. **Ask "Anything to flag?"** Stuck, skipped, took a shortcut, anything. Say it here. Flagging costs you nothing but a redo, and it's exactly what running your own work looks like.
2. **Look at your docs** for today's morning work.
3. **Teach-back:** "What did you learn in this one?", one question about the topic, and "say this sentence of yours in different words".

**How to prepare:** before 13:00, reread what you wrote this morning. Can you say each answer out loud without looking? If not, read that part again.

If the morning isn't done by the checkpoint, you'll do the afternoon side by side with your parent. That's support, not punishment: it goes back to normal after two self-run days.

At 17:00 the close: "Anything to flag?", "What was the most interesting thing today?", a teach-back on the afternoon subjects, and you find out tomorrow's support.

## Self-marking and what counts as a mistake

At 16:00, mark today's work **in green**. Never change the original answer: the mistake stays visible, the correction goes next to it.

**Maths:** check against your self-test answers (Friday) or the scores Khan or Oak gave you. For each wrong answer, find out why from the hint or the video.

**Written answers:** reread the part of the reading the question is about. Mark each answer:

- ✔ it has every point the question asks for, with the reason,
- ½ partly there, or no reason given,
- ✘ wrong or missing.

**For every mistake, write one line:** what kind of mistake it was, and **new** or **again**. "Again" means the same kind showed up before in your doc: look back at earlier days.

**Kinds of mistake:**

| Maths | Written answers |
|---|---|
| Wrong method: didn't know what to do | Missing point: the question asked for something you didn't give |
| Wrong operation: added instead of subtracted, and so on | Wrong fact: a name, date, number or event is wrong |
| Sign or arithmetic slip: the method was right, a calculation wasn't | No reason: said what, but not why |
| Place value or column slip | No example, or an example not from the reading |
| Misread the question | Misread the question |
| No working: right answer, no steps shown | Muddled: the right ideas, but someone else couldn't follow it |
| Didn't check the answer | Spelling and grammar (English only) |

**Not mistakes, but shortcuts:** copying a sentence from the reading without quote marks, opening the answers before you finished, pasting anything. Those you flag at the checkpoint, not mark.

*Why this matters: a mistake you've found yourself is the cheapest lesson there is. "Again" mistakes are the most useful ones: they show you exactly what to practise.*

## Stuck?

1. Reread the part, slowly. Find the sentence where you lost it.
2. Look for an example in the reading or the video, and try it yourself.
3. Still stuck after 10 minutes: write in your doc what you tried and what exactly you don't understand. Then move on to the next part.
4. Flag it at the checkpoint.

Being stuck means you've reached the edge of what you know. That's where learning happens. Hiding it wastes the moment.

# Free literacy sources

## Where to get them

- [Project Gutenberg Young Adult Fiction (Goodreads list)](https://www.goodreads.com/list/show/44192.Project_Gutenberg_Young_Adult_Fiction): young adult fiction available free as Project Gutenberg ebooks.
- Project Gutenberg (gutenberg.org): the main free ebook library.
- Standard Ebooks (standardebooks.org): the same public-domain books, carefully edited and formatted. Nicer to read than Gutenberg.
- LibriVox (librivox.org): free volunteer-read audiobooks of the same books. Good for reluctant readers, or listening while following the text.
- Internet Archive (archive.org): scans of old translations not on Gutenberg.

## Reading ladder

Ordered by accessibility and enjoyment: start at the top to build the habit, move down for depth. Region in brackets.

Selection bar: avoid books that normalise racism, slavery or colonialism. Caveats are noted where a book is good but imperfect.

Availability: originals are public domain, but English translations carry their own copyright, so only older translations are free. Title links go to the free ebook (checked October 2026); author links go to the author's full Gutenberg page.

### 1. Easy hooks: short, fast, fun

- **[Arthur Conan Doyle](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/69), [*The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1661)** (UK). Self-contained short mysteries; one per sitting. The best entry point on this list.
- **Natsume Sōseki, [*Botchan*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/8868)** (Japan). Funny short novel about a hot-headed young teacher at a provincial school. Teen-relatable.
- **Horacio Quiroga, [*South American Jungle Tales*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46051)** (Uruguay). Animal adventures in the jungle; very short stories.
- **H. G. Wells, [*The Time Machine*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/35)** (UK). Short, gripping, raises big questions about society.
- **[*The Arabian Nights Entertainments*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/128), Andrew Lang's selection** (Middle East). Aladdin, Ali Baba, Sinbad in readable form. Some tales use stereotyped Black slave characters; worth a comment.
- **Anna Sewell, [*Black Beauty*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/271)** (UK). A horse's life story; plain language, strong theme of cruelty vs. kindness.
- **[*Korean Tales*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/55539)** (1889) and **[*West African Folk-Tales*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/66923)** (1917) (Korea, West Africa). Short folk stories. The colonial-era introductions to the African collections can be patronising; skip them.

### 2. Gripping and longer

- **Arthur Conan Doyle, [*The Hound of the Baskervilles*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2852)** (UK). Full-length Holmes; the natural next step after the short stories.
- **[L. M. Montgomery](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/36), [*Anne of Green Gables*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45)** (Canada). Talkative, imaginative heroine; warm and funny.
- **[Frances Hodgson Burnett](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/73), [*The Secret Garden*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/113)** (UK). A spoiled, lonely girl changes through a hidden garden. Contains colonial-era remarks about India from Mary early on, framed as part of her bad character.
- **[Louisa May Alcott](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/102), [*Little Women*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/514)** (US). Four sisters growing up; strong on character and family.
- **Robert Louis Stevenson, [*Treasure Island*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/120)** (UK). The classic pirate adventure. Some sailing vocabulary, but the plot carries it.
- **Robert Louis Stevenson, [*Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/42)** (UK). Short, dark, a mystery with a twist.
- **H. G. Wells, [*The War of the Worlds*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36)** (UK). Alien invasion; partly a critique of colonialism from the invaded side.
- **Jules Verne, [*Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/164)** (France). Submarine adventure. Skip *Around the World in Eighty Days* (stereotyped portrayals).
- **Baroness Orczy, [*The Scarlet Pimpernel*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60)** (UK/Hungary). Secret identity and rescues during the French Revolution.
- **E. Nesbit, [*The Railway Children*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1874)** (UK). Children coping after their father disappears. Some of her other books have dated depictions.
- **Jerome K. Jerome, [*Three Men in a Boat*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/308)** (UK). Comic boat trip on the Thames; humour still works.
- **Kate Douglas Wiggin, [*Rebecca of Sunnybrook Farm*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/498)** (US). Lively, Anne-like heroine.
- **Charles Dickens, [*A Christmas Carol*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46)** (UK). Short; the easiest way into Dickens.
- **Frederick Douglass, [*Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/23)** (US). Short, powerful autobiography of escape from slavery. Pairs with history.
- **Zitkala-Ša, [*American Indian Stories*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10376)** (US, Dakota). Short pieces about her childhood and the boarding schools that tried to erase her culture.
- **Rabindranath Tagore, [*The Hungry Stones, and Other Stories*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2518)** (India). Atmospheric short stories, some ghostly.
- **Rabindranath Tagore, [*The Crescent Moon*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6520)** (India). Short poems about childhood; quick to read.
- **[*Brazilian Tales*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/21040)** (1921) (Brazil). Short stories, including Machado de Assis, Brazil's greatest novelist.
- **Ryūnosuke Akutagawa, [*Tales Grotesque and Curious*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/78105)** (Japan). Short, sharp, morally ambiguous; great for nuance.
- **Kahlil Gibran, [*The Madman*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5616)** (Lebanon). Very short parables; read one, discuss one.

### 3. Rewarding with effort

- **Alexandre Dumas, [*The Three Musketeers*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1257)** (France). Swordfights, loyalty, intrigue. Dumas's father was a Black French general.
- **Jane Austen, [*Pride and Prejudice*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1342)** (UK). Sharp, funny social comedy once the style clicks.
- **Charles Dickens, [*Great Expectations*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1400)** (UK). Long but strongly plotted. Skip *Oliver Twist* (antisemitic caricature).
- **Ibn Tufail, [*Hayy ibn Yaqzan*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16831)** (Andalusia, 12th c.). A boy alone on an island works out the world by reasoning. Free version is a 1708 translation (*The Improvement of Human Reason*), so stiff; strong for systems thinking.
- **Kahlil Gibran, [*The Prophet*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/58585)** (Lebanon). Short reflective essays on life; dip in rather than read straight through.
- **Olaudah Equiano, [*The Interesting Narrative*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15399)** (Nigeria/Britain, 1789). Kidnapped, enslaved, freed; abolitionist autobiography. 18th-century prose.
- **Kartini, [*Letters of a Javanese Princess*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34647)** (Indonesia). A young Javanese woman on education, tradition and colonial rule. The best free Indonesian voice.
- **Charles W. Chesnutt, [*The Wife of His Youth and Other Stories of the Color Line*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11057)** (US). Black American author, around 1900; irony and the colour line.
- **Laozi, [*Tao Te Ching*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/216), and Confucius, [*Analects*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3330)** (China), Legge translations. Short sayings, deep content; good for paradoxical thinking. Dip-in reading.

### 4. Stretch

- **Alexandre Dumas, [*The Count of Monte Cristo*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1184)** (France). Revenge epic; very long. Start with an abridged version.
- **José Rizal, [*Noli Me Tangere*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6737)** (Philippines), translated as *The Social Cancer*. Anti-colonial novel that shaped a national movement.
- **Rabindranath Tagore, [*The Home and the World*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/7166)** (India). Politics, nationalism and loyalty inside a marriage.
- **[*Romance of the Three Kingdoms*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/77416)** (China), Brewitt-Taylor translation. Huge historical epic of war and strategy. Only volume 1 of 2 is on Gutenberg so far.
- **Multatuli, [*Max Havelaar*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/70128)** (Netherlands/Java). Dutch author exposing colonial abuse in Java. Not an Indonesian voice, but historically important.
- **Sol Plaatje, *Mhudi*** (South Africa). Early novel by a Black South African writer. No free copy online (borrow-only on Internet Archive). His [*Native Life in South Africa*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1452) is free: non-fiction on the 1913 Land Act.
- **Thomas Mofolo, [*Chaka*](https://archive.org/details/chakahistoricalr00mofo)** (Lesotho), 1931 translation, scanned on Internet Archive. Novel about the Zulu king; ambition and power.
- **Rumi and Hafez** (Persia): [*The Persian Mystics: Rumi*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45159), Gertrude Bell's [*Poems from the Divan of Hafiz*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/74883). Poetry in older translations; dip-in.

### Spanish-language originals

If the kids read Spanish, Gutenberg has many Spanish and Latin American originals, which widens the choice a lot.

- **José Martí, [*La Edad de Oro*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/19898)** (Cuba). A magazine he wrote for children; Spanish only.

### Skipped by the selection bar

- Kipling, Rider Haggard, Edgar Rice Burroughs (*Tarzan*), Hugh Lofting (*Doctor Dolittle*), Defoe (*Robinson Crusoe*), Booth Tarkington: racism built into the stories.
- Borderline: Twain's *Huckleberry Finn* (anti-racist intent, full of slurs; better read together), *Jane Eyre* (treatment of Bertha Mason), Jack London (*The Call of the Wild* is fine; his other writing isn't).

Public domain varies by country. Everyone listed died long ago, except H. G. Wells (died 1946), who is not yet free in life-plus-80 countries such as Spain.

# Year 8 Curriculum Guide (UK)

Condensed from one UK secondary school's Year 8 Curriculum Booklet (27 pages), weighted toward the three National Curriculum core subjects — English, Mathematics, Science. Geography, History and Modern Foreign Languages are kept fuller than the remaining foundation subjects. This is a real school's syllabus, not a generic national-curriculum summary.

All subjects at the school are organised around a termly "fertile question" rather than a topic list — each unit of work is framed as a question students answer by the end of term, assessed against it.

## Core subjects

### English — 2×30min homework/week

"Whole game of English": Reading, Writing, Oracy, taught together every unit.

| Term | Fertile question | Content | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autumn 1 | In a good novel is everyone miserable? Does war bring out the best in people? | *Of Mice and Men* (upper band) / *Once* (lower band) — character analysis | Essay on how the author uses a named character to explore loneliness/kindness/hope |
| Autumn 2 | Does writing have the power to transport? | Extracts across time periods; descriptive writing | Original descriptive piece set in a specific historical moment |
| Spring 1 | Are we all insane? | Heritage + modern poetry, interesting/deviant characters | Comparative essay on two poems |
| Spring 2 | Are the robots taking over? | Non-fiction viewpoint texts on technology | Argumentative piece on technology's impact |
| Summer 1 | Is it better to be dead than alone? | *Romeo and Juliet* — types of love | Essay on Shakespeare's presentation of love / the ending as tragic |

Two homeworks/week: a pre-reading comprehension task, and prep for the week's marked piece. Resources: BBC KS3 Bitesize, BBC Skillswise, grammar-monster.com.

### Mathematics — 2×30min homework/week (one written, one digital via MyMaths or hegartymaths.com)

| Term | Focus |
|---|---|
| Autumn 1 | Number — rounding, calculator use, index laws, primes, factors, multiples |
| Autumn 2 | Shape — area and circumference of circles and circular shapes, volume |
| Spring 1 | Algebra — solving equations/inequations, rearranging formulae, constructing equations |
| Spring 2 | Number — percentage change, multipliers, compound interest |
| Summer 1 | Probability — calculating probabilities, listing systematically, set theory & notation |
| Summer 2 | Synoptic revision — full Y8 content |

Assessment: two 55-minute papers every half term (one fluency, one application), explicitly GCSE/Cambridge-entrance-style. Stretch is harder questions on current content, not racing ahead — "think like a mathematician rather than merely prioritising speed."

### Science — 1×30min homework/week

Content is framed backward from GCSE/A-level top-grade requirements.

| Term | Fertile question |
|---|---|
| Autumn 1 | Would we be able to survive without plants? |
| Autumn 2 | How accurate are the Star Wars films? |
| Spring 1 | Can all mixtures be separated? |
| Spring 2 | Does everyone have an equal right to a heart transplant? (ethics/applications of science) |
| Summer 1 | Is electricity always dangerous? |
| Summer 2 | Should we be forced to recycle? |

Resources: BBC KS3 Bitesize Science, CGP KS3 Science Study Guide (ISBN 978-1-84146-230-1).

## Humanities & languages

### Geography — 30min homework/week

Ecosystems ("the toughest tribe"), global manufacturing and ethics ("take your conscience shopping"), migration and conflict ("a world without war"), weather hazards, development economics ("money money money"), coasts. Assessment: GCSE-style short answer and data-response questions, plus evaluated fieldwork.

### History — 30min homework/week + a holiday stretch project

English Civil War ("is change always for the best?"), British Empire interpretations ("why did the sun not set for 200 years?"), Industrial Revolution ("did Britain make the modern world?"), slavery ("could progress in Britain have been possible without inequality?"), US Civil Rights ("did life improve after slavery?"), then a revision term. Skills emphasis: recall/select/communicate knowledge, historical terminology, using sources to understand differing interpretations.


### Modern Foreign Languages (French or German, continuing from Year 7) — 30min homework/week

Four skills weighted equally, each term built around a fertile question with a cultural theme attached.

- **French:** stereotypes (Au1) → past tense / "what have you done" (Au2) → peer pressure (Sp1) → food & drink (Sp2) → future tense / summer plans (Su1) → consolidation + a French text or film (Su2)
- **German:** past tense (Au1) → shopping/consumerism (Au2) → popular culture (Sp1) → health & the body (Sp2) → peer pressure (Su1) → consolidation + a German text or film (Su2)

Strong Year 7 German students (reading age above biological age) may add French as an optional, ungraded morning-reading-time class. Resources: Vocab Express (vocabulary practice, individual logins), wordreference.com (French), dict.leo.org (German).

## Other foundation subjects (compressed)

| Subject | Term-by-term focus | Assessment | Homework |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art | Fine art portraits (Au) → 3D clay (Sp) → fine art architecture (Su) | 55-min practical realisation each term | 30min/week, sketchbook |
| Computer Science | Hardware basics (Au) → coding in Python/Logo/Scratch (Sp) → data representation (Su) | GCSE-style termly assessment + half-termly check-ins, all on-computer | 30min/week, on-computer only |
| Design & Technology | Rotates Textile / Product / Food & Nutrition in ~12-week blocks, weekly fertile-question topics within each | Assessment week + review week per rotation | 30min/week |
| Dance | Motif development (Au) → Musicals: Hairspray/Aladdin/Matilda (Sp) → Christopher Bruce's "Swansong" + appreciation (Su) | Performance each term + written exam in summer | None formal |
| Drama | *Oliver Twist* — characterisation/staging (Au) → devising (Sp) → *Hamlet* (Su1) → Stanislavski, melodrama vs. naturalism (Su2) | Practical + written, filmed performances, end-of-year written exam | None formal |
| Music | Blues/keyboards (Au) → African drumming/djembe (Sp) → Minimalism/music tech (Su) | Practical assessment each term | None formal (3×/week practice encouraged) |
| PE | Rotates rugby/netball, basketball/dance, dance/swimming, swimming/athletics, athletics/rounders, football/gymnastics (boys/girls split), plus theory (fundamental skills, goal setting, factors affecting performance, fitness testing, hazards in sport) | GCSE-style practical moderation + half-termly written paper | None formal (half-termly revision pack) |
| PSHE | Relationships & family roles (Au1) → bullying, prejudice, extremism (Au2) → diversity & discrimination (Sp1) → entrepreneurship & financial risk (Sp2) → health incl. relationships/consent/substance use (Su1) → keeping safe incl. basic emergency aid (Su2) | Informal — questionnaires/presentations | None |
| Religious Education | God without religion? (Au1) → Sikhism (Au2) → Islam & the Five Pillars (Sp1) → life after death (Sp2) → ancient philosophy (Su1) → philosophy skills + exam (Su2) | 50-min/15-mark extended writing each term; 2-hour GCSE-style exam in Su2 | 30min/week |

## Weekly homework load (for calibration)

Summed from the above: English 60min + Maths 60min + Science 30min + Geography 30min + History 30min + MFL 30min + Art 30min + Computer Science 30min + D&T 30min + RE 30min ≈ **5.5 hours/week** of formal homework, before PE/PSHE/Music/Dance/Drama (mostly no formal homework, but PE expects a half-termly revision pack and Music expects 3×/week practice). That's the real school's own pacing, useful as a calibration reference.

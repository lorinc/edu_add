# 9th Grade Curriculum Guide

What American 9th graders typically study in English, math, science, social studies, world language, PE, and electives, with sources for teaching each subject at home.

## A Typical Freshman Course Load

Most states require around 22 to 24 total credits to graduate high school, spread over four years. Freshman year usually covers 6 to 7 of those credits, across the subjects below. Exact titles and credit values vary by state and program.

- English 9 (English I): 1 credit
- Math, Algebra I or Geometry: 1 credit
- Science, Biology (lab): 1 credit
- Social Studies, World History or World Geography: 1 credit
- World Language, Spanish, French, Latin, etc.: 1 credit
- PE and Health: 0.5 to 1 credit
- Elective, student's choice: 0.5 to 1 credit

Placement note: a student who finished Algebra I in 8th grade typically takes Geometry as a freshman instead.

## Before You Start, Check Your State's Rules

Homeschool requirements vary a lot by state. Some states ask for nothing beyond your decision to homeschool. Others require annual notice, a minimum number of instructional days or hours, specific subjects taught, and yearly testing or portfolio review.

- 11 states require no notification to any government office to begin homeschooling.
- The five most regulated states, Massachusetts, New York, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, and Vermont, require notification plus evaluations or standardized testing.
- Example of a moderate state: Kentucky asks for an annual notice of attendance and at least 1,062 instructional hours over 170 or more days, covering specific subjects.
- Example of a lighter state: Maine asks for a notice of intent, 175 instructional days, and an annual assessment.

The Home School Legal Defense Association (HSLDA) tracks each state's requirements and updates them as laws change. Check it directly before finalizing a plan; see Sources at the end of this document.

## English

*1 credit, full year*

- Reading literature, novels, drama, and poetry, and informational texts at the grade 9-10 complexity band, with close analysis of theme, character, structure, and point of view
- Argumentative, informative/explanatory, and narrative writing built on evidence and structured reasoning
- Grammar, usage, and vocabulary taught in the context of real reading and writing, not isolated drills
- Speaking, listening, and discussion skills (Socratic seminar, debate, presentations)
- A mix of texts: many programs pair contemporary works like Of Mice and Men and The Taming of the Shrew with classics such as To Kill a Mockingbird, Romeo and Juliet, and The Odyssey

Standards: Common Core Reading, Literature grades 9-10, and Writing grades 9-10 (see Sources).

Free resources: Fishtank Learning's 9th Grade ELA (a complete, free, downloadable year of lesson plans), Project Gutenberg (free full texts of public domain classics), and Easy Peasy All in One High School (a free full year English course).

Structured and paid options: Master Books' 9th grade curriculum (literature based, includes English) and Time4Learning's 9th grade courses (self paced, online, includes English).

## Math

*1 credit, full year*

- Algebra I, the standard freshman placement: linear and quadratic equations and inequalities, functions, exponents and polynomials, systems of equations, and an intro to statistics and data
- Geometry, for students who completed Algebra I in 8th grade: formal proofs, triangle congruence and similarity, the Pythagorean theorem, circles, area and volume, and an introduction to trigonometry
- Either path builds the algebra and geometry foundation that the SAT, ACT, and college level math all assume

Standards: Common Core Mathematics standards, specifically the High School Algebra domain (see Sources).

Free resources: Khan Academy's Algebra 1 course (full mastery based course with video lessons and practice sets) and CK-12 FlexBooks (free, customizable digital math textbooks by topic).

Structured and paid options: Sonlight's 9th grade math guide (placement help for Algebra 1, Geometry, and beyond) and BJU Press Homeschool (full subject textbooks and video taught courses).

## Science

*1 credit, full year, lab based*

- Cell structure and function, plus basic biochemistry (macromolecules, enzymes)
- Genetics and heredity, DNA and protein synthesis
- Evolution and natural selection
- Classification and taxonomy
- Ecology and ecosystems: energy flow, population dynamics, human impact
- Human body systems and physiology
- A hands on lab component: microscope work, dissection, and household experiments are standard even in homeschool programs

Standards: Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), high school life science topics (see Sources).

Free resources: CK-12's Biology FlexBook (free textbook and topic level concepts) and Easy Peasy All in One High School (includes free lab science courses).

Structured and paid options: Apologia's Exploring Creation with Biology (widely used, lab based homeschool biology course, Christian worldview) and Apologia's full high school science line (compare biology, chemistry, and physics options by year).

## Social Studies

*1 credit, full year*

- This is the subject that varies most by state and program. Freshman year usually lands on either World Geography or World History, sometimes paired with Civics
- World Geography track: physical and human geography by continent, climate and ecosystems, maps and GIS tools, world cultures, and current global events
- World History track: a conceptual approach to major global transformations of the 18th, 19th, and 20th centuries
- There is no single national standard here the way Common Core covers English and math. States set their own social studies standards, so check your state department of education

Free resources: Khan Academy's World History content (free video courses) and PBS LearningMedia (free videos, maps, and lesson plans).

Structured and paid options: Notgrass History's Exploring World History and Exploring World Geography, Monarch's World Geography (AOP, full year online course), and Abeka's Grade 9 World Geography (one semester course).

## World Language

*1 credit, full year*

- Foundational vocabulary, grammar, conversation, reading, and cultural context in the chosen language
- Most states average around two required credits of world language, and colleges generally want to see two or more consecutive years of the same language. Freshman year is the common starting point
- Common choices: Spanish, French, Latin, and German
- Not every language program is set up to award an official high school credit, so confirm that before committing to one

Free resources: PBS LearningMedia's world language resources.

Structured and paid options: BJU Press Homeschool (structured Spanish, French, and Latin courses) and Outschool (live small group conversation classes in dozens of languages).

Background reading: iHomeschool Network's guide to earning foreign language credit and Homeschool.com's guide to homeschooling high school foreign language.

## PE and Health

*0.5 to 1 credit, flexible*

- Most states require just one credit of PE to graduate. Check your state's exact minimum
- Health content typically covers personal fitness and nutrition, safety, and mental and emotional wellness. PE typically requires a documented log of regular physical activity
- Homeschoolers have real flexibility here: an organized sport, dance, martial arts, or a personal fitness plan can all count toward the credit, not just a textbook course
- A CPR and First Aid certification (American Red Cross or American Heart Association) is a practical, documentable option some families use for part of the health credit

Free resources: Freedom Homeschooling's Health and PE curriculum and 7 Sisters' guide to what counts as PE credit.

Structured and paid options: Thrive, Health and PE for Homeschoolers (a 36 week course built for grades 9 to 12, can count as 1 full credit).

## Electives

*0.5 to 1 credit, student's choice*

Popular freshman year options:

- Computer science and coding: intro programming, game design, robotics
- Art and design: drawing, painting, digital design, graphic design
- Photography and digital media
- Music, theater, or film production
- Personal finance or business basics
- An extra year of world language, beyond the required one

Free resources: Khan Academy (free intro computer programming, arts and humanities, and life skills content).

Structured and paid options: Outschool (140,000+ live small group classes covering coding, art, film, robotics, and more).

## Free Resources That Cover Almost Everything

A handful of sites do double or triple duty across subjects. Most families combine two or three of these with a library card to cover most of the year at little to no cost.

- Khan Academy: free K to 12 courses in math, science, English, history, and more
- CK-12 Foundation: free, standards aligned digital textbooks for middle and high school math and science
- Easy Peasy All in One High School: a complete free high school course of study, math through calculus, five lab sciences, English, history, PE, and more
- PBS LearningMedia: free videos, lesson plans, and games for ELA, math, science, social studies, arts, and world languages
- Project Gutenberg: 75,000+ free full texts of public domain literature
- Homeschool Picks' free curriculum list: a roundup of 25 free homeschool programs across every subject

This is a general planning reference, not a substitute for your state's official homeschool statute or your local school district's requirements. Always confirm current rules directly with your state before finalizing a course of study. Compiled September 2026.

# Sherlock Holmes: the 12 questions

By the end of the topic you must be able to answer all 12 from memory, in your own words. The topic test picks 4, handwritten, no book. For questions that ask for a quotation, learn a short one (a few words is enough) or describe the moment precisely.

**The Red-Headed League**

1. Retell the plot of *The Red-Headed League* in no more than 8 sentences, in the order things actually happened (not the order the story tells them).
2. When Jabez Wilson arrives, Holmes says several things about him without being told. Choose two: what clue did Holmes see, and what did he infer from it?
3. Why did the criminals invent the Red-Headed League? Explain how the whole plan worked.
4. Holmes taps the pavement in front of the pawnbroker's shop and looks at the assistant's knees. What was he checking, and what did it tell him?
5. Describe Watson's role in the story. Why does Conan Doyle tell the story through Watson rather than Holmes?

**The Blue Carbuncle**

6. List three things Holmes infers from the old hat, and the clue behind each.
7. Holmes says the hat's owner is "highly intellectual" because the hat is large. Is that good reasoning? Explain why or why not, and what it tells you about reading any expert's conclusions.
8. Trace the journey of the blue carbuncle from the hotel room to the goose, and from the goose to Holmes.
9. At the end, Holmes lets the thief go. What reasons does he give? Do you agree with his decision? Give your reasons.

**Across both stories, and your writing**

10. What is the difference between observing and inferring? Give one example of each from either story, and one from your own life.
11. How does Conan Doyle build suspense? Name two techniques he uses, with an example of each from the stories.
12. In your own deduction scene, which inference was wrong, and how did your detective realise it? What does this show about how real reasoning differs from Holmes's?

# Time is invented: the 12 questions

By the end of the topic you must be able to answer all 12 from memory, in your own words. The topic test picks 4, handwritten, no notes.

**Part 1. Hours**

1. Why do we have 60 minutes in an hour and 24 hours in a day? Name the peoples involved.
2. What were "seasonal hours", and what made Europe switch to equal hours?

**Part 2. The week**

3. The week, unlike the day, month and year, comes from nowhere in nature. Where does the seven-day week come from? Give the two roots, and explain where two English day names come from.
4. Revolutionary France and the Soviet Union both tried to change the week. What did each one try, why, and why did they fail?

**Part 3. Clocks**

5. Who first needed mechanical clocks in medieval Europe, and why? How did the use of clocks change when towns built clock towers?

**Part 4. Railways**

6. Why did railways need a single national time? Explain what "local time" was, using the example of Bristol and London.
7. Who decided the time zones of the United States in 1883, and what does that tell you about who has the power to set time?

**Part 5. Factories**

8. What is the difference between task-oriented work and timed work? Give an example of each.
9. Summarise E. P. Thompson's argument about time discipline in the Industrial Revolution, and one criticism other historians make of it.

**Part 6. Other clocks**

10. Why do many teenagers struggle to fall asleep early and wake early? What does this suggest about school start times?
11. Give two examples where a government set the clock for political reasons, and explain the reason in each case.

**Applying it**

12. Using what you've learned, explain one way you would organise your own time differently, and why. Say what it would cost you, and who else it would affect.

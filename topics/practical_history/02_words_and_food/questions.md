# Words and food are fossils: the 12 questions

By the end of the topic you must be able to answer all 12 from memory, in your own words. The topic test picks 4, handwritten, no notes.

**Part 1. How words travel**

1. Give three reasons why one language borrows words from another, with an example of each.
2. Most words in an English dictionary come from French and Latin, but most words people actually say come from Old English. Explain how both can be true.

**Part 2. 1066**

3. Explain the cow/beef pattern and the usual explanation for it. Then say what's true about it and what's too neat.
4. Why did English borrow pronouns (they, them, their) from the Vikings but not from the Normans? What does that tell you about the two kinds of contact?

**Part 3. Arabic**

5. Choose three English words that come from Arabic. For each, explain its origin and what it tells you about who was leading in science or trade at the time.
6. Trace the journey of the numbers we use (0-9) from where they were invented to Europe. Why do people disagree about what to call them?

**Part 4. The world inside English**

7. Why do some languages say "tea" and others "cha"? Explain how a word can tell you a trade route.
8. Give two English words that record the British Empire in India or the conquest of the Americas, and explain what each one reveals.

**Part 5. The Columbian exchange**

9. What was the Columbian exchange? Give three things that went each way, and one dish from today that couldn't exist without it.
10. The potato blight was natural. Explain why historians say the Irish famine was not purely a natural disaster.

**Part 6. Food and violence**

11. Choose two foods from Part 6 and explain the violence or power behind each.
12. "The exchange enriched the world, and much of it was carried out through violence." Explain both halves of this sentence with examples, and explain why it matters not to pick just one.
